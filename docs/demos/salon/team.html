The chairs

Four people, and you get to pick

Everyone here can cut and colour. What differs is what they're fastest and surest at, so it's worth reading before you book.

Not sure who

A rough guide

Going significantly lighter

Ask for Orla or Nadia. Big lifts on dark or previously coloured hair are where experience genuinely changes the outcome, not just the speed.

Curls, coils or anything short

Theo. He cuts dry more often than not, which is the right approach for texture and for shapes that live or die on a quarter of an inch.

A wedding, or anything with a deadline

Jun for the styling, booked early, with a trial about a month before. Colour for an event should be four weeks ahead, not four days.

Something's gone wrong elsewhere

Nadia, via the free correction consult. Bring photos of what it looked like before and what was used on it, if you have any of that.

Still unsure? Pick no preference when you book. We read the notes you leave and put you with whoever fits, usually sooner than a named stylist could.

Behind the chairs

We train our own

Everyone who works here came up through the floor: two years assisting before they touch a paying client's colour, and a model night every Wednesday after closing that the seniors run unpaid.

That's why the range of experience shows up in the price list rather than being hidden. Paying Stylist rate doesn't mean getting less care; it means getting someone who's been doing it four years instead of sixteen.

Model nights. Get in touch

Wednesday model nights

Cuts and colour at cost, done by assistants under supervision. Takes longer; costs a lot less.

Two years assisting

Shampooing, prepping colour, watching. Nobody touches a client's colour before that.

Ongoing education

Every stylist gets four paid training days a year and picks the courses themselves.

No commission

Nobody here earns more for selling you a treatment. That's deliberate.
