About the label

Twelve things, made properly, for as long as people want them.

Brindle started in 2017 with one tee and a spreadsheet of mills that would talk to someone ordering forty metres. It's now twelve pieces and six people, and the spreadsheet is still how we buy cloth.

Why twelve

A small range is the whole strategy

Making four hundred styles a year means you can't know any of them well. Twelve means we can afford the better cloth, order enough of it to matter to the mill, and keep the same factory busy all year rather than in bursts.

It also means when something goes wrong we know immediately. A batch of the Halden came back in 2023 with a neck tape that stretched; because it was one of twelve things, we caught it in a week and replaced every one sold.

The trade-off is that we're boring. There is no new collection to announce. The same tee has been on this site for eight years.

Where it's made

Four places, all named

"Premium fabric" and "ethically sourced" mean nothing without an address. Here are ours.

Vila do Conde, Portugal

The factory

Everything we sell is cut and sewn here, by a family business of 90 people we've used since the first run of tees. Audited annually by an independent body; the report is a link away if you ask for it.

Hawick, Scotland

The knitwear

Fully fashioned lambswool on machines older than the business. Each panel is knitted to shape rather than cut from a sheet, about 20% less yarn wasted, and no raw edges inside the seams.

Huddersfield, England

The melton

A mill that has been making coating cloth on the same site since the 1830s. Our 750gsm melton is milled tight enough to shed drizzle without any chemical coating.

Okayama, Japan

The denim & oxford

Shuttle looms running at about thirty metres a day. Slower than a modern loom by a factor of ten, which is exactly why the cloth has the irregularity you can feel.

Materials

What we use, and what we don't

Natural first

Cotton, lambswool, alpaca, cupro. No polyester, no elastane, nothing that sheds plastic in the wash. It costs more and it creases; those are the trade-offs and we'd rather state them than hide them.

Fewer treatments

No easy-care resin, no anti-microbial finish, no water repellent coating. A melton tight enough to shed rain doesn't need spraying with anything.

Honest about the gaps

Our thread is polyester, because cotton thread fails first and a seam that bursts is worse than a seam that lasts. Corozo buttons come from Ecuador. We ship by air when a restock is late, which we'd rather not do.
