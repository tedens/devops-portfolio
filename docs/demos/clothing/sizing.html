Size & care

Our sizes are our sizes. Rather than claim they match anyone else's, every product page lists what the garment itself measures, laid flat. This page explains how to use that.

How to measure Size charts Care Returns & repairs

Step one

Measure a garment you already like

Body measurements are a detour. The quickest way to get this right is to take something you own that fits the way you want, lay it flat, and compare it to the numbers on the product page.

  • Chest / bust. Lay it flat, measure straight across from one armhole seam to the other. That's the half-chest. The number we publish.
  • Body length. From the highest point of the shoulder straight down to the hem.
  • Shoulder. Seam to seam across the back.
  • Sleeve. From the shoulder seam to the cuff edge, along the outside.
  • Waist. Trousers flat, buttoned, measured across the top and doubled.
  • Inseam. Crotch seam to hem, along the inside leg.

A tape measure that's gone soft will read long. If in doubt, use a metal one, or a piece of string against a ruler.

What we mean by

Four words that get used badly

Close

Follows the body with no excess. Not tight. You should be able to get a hand in.

Regular

Room to layer a shirt underneath, straight through the waist, no tapering.

Relaxed

Cut to go over knitwear. Dropped shoulder in most cases.

Boxy

Straight from armhole to hem. Wider than it is long, relative to a regular cut.

Care

Wash less, wash colder

Most clothes are destroyed by laundry rather than by wear. Nothing we make needs washing as often as you think. Cotton airs out overnight, and wool essentially cleans itself if you hang it somewhere with moving air.

  • Cotton jersey. Cold, inside out, line dry. Dryers are what shrink a tee, not the wash.
  • Oxford & poplin. Warm is fine. Iron while slightly damp and you'll spend half the time.
  • Lambswool & alpaca. Wool cycle or hand wash, reshape, dry flat. Never hang a wet knit.
  • Raw denim. Cold soak first, then wash rarely, inside out. Fades come from wearing, not washing.
  • Melton wool. Dry clean once a season at most. Brush it instead. A stiff clothes brush does most of the work.
  • Canvas. Spot clean. A wash will soften it, which you may or may not want.

Returns & repairs

The policy, in full

60 days

Worn or unworn, as long as it's clean. Prepaid label in every box. No restocking fee and no form asking you to explain yourself.

Free repairs, for life

Seams, buttons, moth holes, a blown knee, a zip. Post it back and we'll fix it and return it, including postage. There's no receipt to find.

Hemmed free

Trousers ship unhemmed at 82 cm. Give us your inseam in the bag and we'll do it before dispatch. Hemmed trousers are still returnable.
