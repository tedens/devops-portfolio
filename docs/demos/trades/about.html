About

Two vans. One of them is usually me.

Dad started Hollis Electrical in 1998 out of a garage on Fenmore Road. I took it over in 2019, kept the name, the van livery and the rule about never quoting a job we haven't seen.

Why we stay small

Twenty minutes from the yard

We could cover three times the area. We'd also spend half the day in traffic, and we couldn't get back to a customer with no power the same afternoon. So we stay inside a radius we can actually serve.

That's the honest reason the coverage checker sometimes says no. It isn't that your job is too small. It's that we'd be a bad choice for it, and we'd rather say so in five seconds than take a deposit.

Two vans means two jobs a day, most days. It also means when you ring you get somebody who knows your house, not a call centre reading from a script.

How we work

Four things we won't bend on

We look before we quote

Anything beyond a fixed-price job gets a free survey first. A number given over the phone is a number that changes halfway through, and everyone knows it.

We stop if it grows

Find something unexpected and we down tools and tell you the new figure. If it's something the survey should have caught, the original quote stands.

We file the paperwork

Notifiable work gets certified and registered. It's the bit that costs us time and earns us nothing, and it's the bit that matters when you sell.

We say when to do nothing

Plenty of old installations are perfectly safe and don't need replacing because a survey report looks alarming. We'll tell you that, even though the rewire pays better.

Fringe. Tuesdays & Thursdays

44124, 44128, 44130, 44143, 44146. We cover these on the days a van is already heading out that way, so booking takes a little longer. No call-out charge either way.

Outside those ZIPs we'll say no rather than take the work and let you down. For a big job we'll sometimes travel. Ring and ask.

Licensing

Checks worth doing

Our licence and insurance

Licence #EL-44118-02, verifiable with the state board. The number is on the van, the invoice and the bottom of every page here. $2m public liability; we'll email the certificate if you ask, and you should ask, of anyone.

How to check any electrician

Ask for the licence number and check it yourself rather than taking a photo of a card at face value. Ask who registers notifiable work. Ask for the insurance certificate. Anyone legitimate expects all three and won't be offended.

What we don't do

Commercial fit-outs above a small unit, anything involving the supply company's equipment, solar and battery installs, and fire alarm systems that need a specialist certificate. We'll point you at someone who does, and we don't take a referral fee for it.
