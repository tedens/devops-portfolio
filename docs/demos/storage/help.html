Help

The questions we get asked

Straight answers, including the ones about money.

Paying

What do I actually pay on day one?

The part of the first month you're using, the $24 admin fee, the first part-month of cover if you take ours, and a lock if you need one. No deposit, and nothing else.

Move in on the 22nd of a 30-day month and the rent line is nine thirtieths of a month, not a month. Every price on this site is calculated that way, and the reservation page shows the arithmetic.

Is there a deposit?

No. Some operators take one and hold it for months. We don't, and there is no version of your account where money sits with us that you can't see.

What happens when I leave?

Ten days' notice on a monthly unit. We refund the days you don't use on the same daily basis we charged for them, back to the card or account that paid.

Empty it, sweep it, take your lock off. That's the whole process.

Do the six- and twelve-month terms lock me in?

They lock the rate, not you. You still pay monthly and you can still leave with ten days' notice. You just lose the discounted rate if you come back later. We do it this way because a discount that traps you isn't a discount.

Will the rent go up?

On a monthly unit we can raise it with 30 days' written notice, and in practice that's once a year at most. On a six- or twelve-month rate it can't change until the term ends.

Access and security

Can anyone else open my unit?

No. You fit the only lock and hold the only key. We don't keep a copy or a master. If you lose the key we'll cut the lock off for you, with ID, and you fit a new one.

Can I send someone else to collect something?

Yes, if you add them to the tenancy first, in person, by phone with your account password, or by email from the address on the account. They'll get their own gate code, and you can remove it whenever you like.

What's recorded when I visit?

The gate logs which code opened it and when, and the cameras cover the roads, the bay and the corridors, kept 90 days. There are no cameras inside a unit, and none pointed at a unit's interior when the door is up.

Is it insured?

The building is. Your things aren't, unless you insure them. Most home policies extend to goods in storage. Check the wording and bring proof, or take one of our tiers from $11 a month.

Choosing a unit

How do I know what size I need?

Use the planner. It packs your actual furniture into a unit drawn to scale and stops at the smallest one that takes it. If you're between two sizes, take the bigger one for a month. Moving down later is free.

Do I need climate control?

Yes for paper, photographs, artwork, timber and veneered furniture, leather, electronics, instruments, vinyl and anything with a screen or a battery.

No for garden tools, bikes, building materials, plastic-crated stock, metal shelving and most car parts. It adds 22%, and for the right contents that's cheap compared with what humidity does over a winter.

Can I store a car or a motorbike?

Yes, in a drive-up unit, with the tank under a quarter and the battery disconnected. A compact car wants a 10 × 20. A 10 × 15 is 15 ft deep, which fits the car and not the door you need to open.

Can I run a business from the unit?

You can store stock, take deliveries to the office by arrangement, and come and go as often as you like. You can't work in the unit, plug anything in, or have customers meet you there. It's a store, not a workshop, and the fire cover is written on that basis.

What you can and can't store

Fine

  • Household furniture and appliances, drained and dry
  • Business stock, records and equipment
  • Bicycles, tools, garden kit with the fuel drained
  • Vehicles, taxed or on a SORN, in a drive-up unit
  • Instruments, artwork and electronics. Climate controlled

Never

  • Fuel, gas bottles, paint, solvents or anything flammable
  • Fireworks, ammunition or weapons
  • Food, plants, or anything living
  • Waste, tyres or chemicals
  • Anything illegal, or anything that isn't yours

The fuel one catches everyone. A mower, a strimmer, a generator or a motorbike has to come in dry. Not nearly empty. Drained. It's the single most common reason we turn something away at the gate.

Packing it properly

Same-size boxes

Two sizes, not nine. Uniform boxes stack to the ceiling and hold their own weight; assorted ones make a pile that leans.

Heavy low, light high

Books in small boxes, bedding in big ones. A large box of books is a box nobody can lift twice.

Label the side

You'll be looking at a wall of box sides, not tops. Number them and keep a list on your phone.
