The hotel

A shipping office that kept its nerve

Built 1927 for the Harbour Board, empty from 1990, reopened in 2021. Eighteen floors, 122 rooms, and rather less reinvention than the brochure industry would like.

What we kept

  • The terrazzo. Original on every floor from the third up. Chipped in places, and it stays chipped.
  • The lift dials. Brass, mechanical, restored. The lifts behind them are entirely new, which is the right way round.
  • The ceilings. 2.9 metres in a standard room, 3.6 in the penthouses. Nobody builds that now.
  • The walls. 1927 concrete between rooms. You will not hear your neighbour, and that is not a claim we could make about a new build.

What we changed

  • Glass where the loading doors were, on the harbour side of every floor
  • A pool in the strongroom, because the strongroom was already waterproof
  • Ducting and wiring in the old mail chutes, which is why the ceilings survived
  • Two penthouses on the eighteenth, where the plant room used to be

Meetings

The Chart Room

One room, on the second floor, seating fourteen around the table the Harbour Board used to argue across. Daylight on two sides, a screen that works, and a door that shuts properly.

We do not run a conference floor and we are not going to pretend otherwise. If you need to put ninety people in a room, we are the wrong hotel and we will happily tell you which one isn't.

The Quarter

What's within ten minutes on foot

2 min

The harbour steps and the morning fish market, which starts at 5am and is worth one early alarm.

6 min

Quay Street's restaurants, most of them small and most of them needing a table booked.

8 min

The Maritime Museum, in the old customs house. Free on Wednesdays.

10 min

The headland path, which runs four kilometres round to the lighthouse and back.
